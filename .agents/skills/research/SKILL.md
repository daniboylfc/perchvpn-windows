# Research — Evidence-Driven Research

## Purpose
Produce accurate, useful research by separating evidence, interpretation, uncertainty, and unanswered questions.

## Operating Rules
- Define the research question and scope before searching.
- Prefer primary, official, technical, academic, or otherwise authoritative sources when available.
- Use multiple independent sources for consequential claims.
- Check publication dates and distinguish current information from historical information.
- Do not treat search snippets as sufficient evidence when the underlying source is available.
- Distinguish documented facts from analysis, speculation, and opinion.
- Resolve conflicting claims by examining methodology, provenance, date, and supporting evidence.
- Never manufacture citations, sources, statistics, quotations, or consensus.
- State meaningful uncertainty explicitly.
- Stop researching when additional searching is unlikely to materially improve the answer.

## Research Workflow
1. Define question
2. Identify source classes
3. Search broadly
4. Identify authoritative sources
5. Cross-check important claims
6. Resolve conflicts
7. Synthesize
8. Identify uncertainty and gaps
9. Produce concise conclusions with supporting evidence

## Output
When appropriate, structure results as: Executive Summary, Findings, Evidence, Conflicting Information, Uncertainty, and Practical Next Steps.
